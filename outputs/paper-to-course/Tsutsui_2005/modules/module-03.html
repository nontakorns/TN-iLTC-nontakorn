Ways to decide who gets care

Japan's choice against the options the paper names: old Japanese practice, US case-mix, professional judgment, and Canadian single-entry systems.

Two ways to measure need

The paper frames the choice simply. Public LTC has to either estimate care needs directly (a professional judges what the person needs) or assess physical and mental status and estimate care needs from it. Japan took the second route: it scores body and mind, then converts the score into minutes of care with a statistical model.

Why Japan said no to RUG-III

📉
Poor fit

It did not explain Japanese resource-use patterns. In the US, therapist-led rehab is among the costliest NH services and drives RUG groups. Japanese NHs rarely had physical therapists: no pilot resident had a 30-minute individual rehab session.

👩‍⚕️
Workforce

RUG-III needs nurses specially trained in the MDS. Japan could not train enough of them before launch.

💡
What it left behind

The ideas of patient/resident classification and prospective payment, which shaped Japanese LTC.

Germany and Israel let professionals decide. Why not you?

MHLW planner

That's what we had locally, and people called it arbitrary. Criteria varied by area, and politics got independent seniors into homes.

Time-study team

Then let the data decide. We observed 10 million minutes of care. A model can turn a questionnaire any trained official can fill in into minutes of care.

MH
MHLW planner

Agreed. But keep a professional board to review every result.
