The decision rule

The paper gives no equation. Below is Claude's schematic of the logic it describes: per-category tree-regression estimates, summed, then cut at fixed thresholds.

Schematic (Claude's reconstruction)
T = Σk=1..9 treek(s1, …, s7, m)  →  Level = cut(T; 25, 30, 50, 70, 90, 110)
What it means
s₁ … s₇The applicant's standardized scores on the 7 physical/mental dimensions.
mUse of the 12 medical procedures.
treek( )A regression tree for care category k. It predicts how many minutes per day an "ideal" institution would spend on this person. Trees were fitted on the 1995 time-study data.
k = 1..9Nine care categories. The paper lists: grooming/bathing, eating, toileting, transferring, IADL assistance, behavioural problems, rehabilitation, medical services. "Eating" appears twice, so only 8 are named; the ninth is unclear.
TTotal estimated care minutes per day. This is the standardized unit of need (principle 3).
cut( )Thresholds from Table 2. Exception: under 25 minutes still gets "need support" if estimated rehab or IADL time exceeds 10 minutes.

Hana's journey (Appendix 1)

Hana, 88, has severe arthritis and heart failure and lives with her eldest son and his wife. The steps follow the paper's appendix; the dialogue is invented.

Ha
Hana

I need help walking, toileting, bathing and changing clothes. I've called the city.

PH
Public health nurse (assessor)

I'll visit and go through the questionnaire: movement, daily activities, memory, behaviour, medical procedures. I'll add my own notes.

PC
Computer model

Scores in, minutes estimated across the care categories. The total sets a provisional level, which goes to the board.

CB
Certification Board

With the physician's statement and the assessor's notes in front of us, we certify Level 2. That gives a ceiling of $1,821 a month for home services. She hears back within a month.

CM
Care manager

Proposed plan: home help 1 hour twice a week ($301), bathing once a month ($117), wheelchair rental ($50), care plan ($80).

Ha
Hana

My family will help with the rest, so I'll stay below the maximum and keep my copayment low, about $55 a month. Now I can get around the house on my own in the wheelchair.

Arithmetic check (Claude)

The four itemised fees add up to $548, but the paper reports a total of $553. The $55 copayment matches 10% of either figure. The paper does not say whether the care-plan fee carries a copayment.
