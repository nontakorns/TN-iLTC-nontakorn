# LTC eligibility and transition evidence

**For TN-iLTC WP3, start with Japanese care-needs certification, the two Michigan MI Choice papers, and Korean certification-component validation; then use MAPLe to distinguish eligibility from prioritization.** These are the practical first five readings for the 15 October 2026 search-strategy workshop. A ten-paper seed set adds transitional home-care resources, disability-service assessment, acquired-brain-injury support needs, a pediatric sentinel, and Thai prognosis. It contains **four direct eligibility/certification papers, including one component study; five adjacent care-needs/prioritization papers; and one contextual prediction paper**. They are search seeds, not ten studies already judged eligible for the eventual review. The strongest lesson is to identify the decision each instrument supports before comparing its performance. This focused evidence review does not establish a Thai LTC threshold or a rule for ending IMC.

Prepared for the merged Q1, need-based LTC eligibility, and Q2, entry after Thai intermediate care, using the team's supplied 8 October scope: all ages, with protocol decisions retained by the WP3 team. Evidence checked through **12 October 2026**. This is a critical research briefing, not a completed systematic review or a final protocol.

## Ten seeds expose different care decisions

The first five papers cover the central methodological contrast: an implemented certification process, a service-level screen, a screen's limitations, setting-specific component validity, and a priority algorithm. Reading them first should help the team specify what it means by an “eligibility tool.” The remaining five deliberately test the boundaries of the all-age and post-IMC question. ## Certification studies validate different reference standards

Japan's historical certification paper is the most useful Asian starting point. Its initial algorithm drew on institutional professional-care time and was tested in **175,129 older participants** in national model programs; professional boards reviewed the initial classification. This describes development and system implementation, rather than an independent diagnostic-accuracy estimate. The **85-item historical version** must not be conflated with later certification instruments. Use this seed to retrieve the original Japanese statistical studies and to ask whose judgment defined “need.” ([Tsutsui and Muramatsu](https://agsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2005.53175.x))

MI Choice offers a different model: screening for publicly funded home/community services and an appropriate care level. In the development abstract, approximately 30 characteristics achieved **κ=0.62** against expert care-manager judgments, compared with **κ below 0.40** for ADL/IADL-only systems. This supports examining multidomain screening, but expert agreement is not proof of improved outcomes or objectively correct entitlement. ([Fries et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12145374/)) The telephone follow-up found only marginal agreement with in-person assessment: telephone respondents were classified as more impaired in **27%** and less impaired in **6%**. The authors supported broad targeting but rejected telephone screening alone for final medical eligibility or a specific care level. For Thailand, the transferable hypothesis is a screen-to-assessment workflow, subject to local testing. ([Fries et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15498843/))

The Korean component study is especially useful for challenging care-minute reference standards. In **407 non-dementia older LTCI users**, motor impairment related to observed ADL service time in facilities but not significantly among domiciliary users; adjusted explained variance was **0.33 versus 0.024**, respectively. A scale can therefore behave differently across service settings. Delivered minutes also depend on staffing, service availability and informal help, so they should not automatically be treated as the amount of care a person ought to receive. This is validity evidence for one certification component, not validation of the entire Korean eligibility system. ([Kim et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3713298/))

Two supplementary papers deserve prominent attention when defining the reference standard. The ZEVIOUS study compared Japanese care levels with self-reported functioning in **181 home medical-care patients**: correlation with WHODAS-12 was **0.58**, with substantial variation within levels. Questionnaire completers cannot represent everyone with severe cognitive or communication problems. This is construct validity, not cutoff accuracy. ([Tsugihashi et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33393206/)) Shanghai investigators reclassified **179,141 assessments** using national versus local standards and obtained different grade distributions, particularly around cognition. Without an independent gold standard, this demonstrates consequences of changing rules rather than identifying the correct rule. ([Wan et al. 2024](https://hdpr.shdrc.org/CN/abstract/article/2097-4205/3772))

The central interpretation is that **certification is a policy-defined decision, not a fixed clinical truth**. When the comparator is a committee, an existing grade, expert opinion, care minutes or service receipt, the study answers a different question. WP3 should record these separately and avoid pooling κ, construct correlations and classification accuracy as interchangeable “validity.” A threshold can change because policy priorities change, even if the underlying patient characteristics do not.

## Postacute studies support reassessment and broader need domains

MAPLe is a useful boundary paper because its output is priority. Derived from routine Ontario home-care assessments and evaluated in several jurisdictions, its five levels combine clinical needs with markers including caregiver distress and placement risk. Separate Ontario follow-up found one-year nursing-home admission of **4.8% at low priority versus 47.4% at very high priority**. This does not turn MAPLe into an entitlement rule: admission also reflects caregiver capacity and the local care system. Its value for WP3 is to distinguish high need, urgency, caregiver strain and service destination. ([MAPLe full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-6-9))

The transitional RUG-III/HC study directly addresses a hospital-to-home program context, although Canadian programs are not Thai IMC. It examined **1,680 completed episodes** in eight 10–16-week programs, with smaller usable billing subsets. Classification explained roughly **22–23% of combined paid/unpaid care-time variance**. This is modest resource prediction, not eligibility validation. It argues for testing whether a long-stay classification works in transitional care, while reminding WP3 that billing data capture delivered resources and organizational practice. ([Bolster-Foucault and Holyoke](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-10150-1))

For a more direct measure of continuing help, the Northwick Park family is a valuable supplemental trail. NPCNA converts nursing dependency into a community care timetable and estimated hours; its initial validation compared **35 cases** with an independent structured interview. ([Turner-Stokes et al. 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10392653/)) A companion postacute study followed **39 brain-injury patients**, with only 15 assessed at three months in the community. Median weekly care hours fell **52 to 17** during rehabilitation. That supports responsiveness and reassessment, with a small follow-up denominator; it does not show that disability becomes permanent at an administrative endpoint. ([Nyein et al. 1999](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026921599674590637))

CANS complements care-hour approaches by measuring support needs. The 2010 study included **108 people aged 16–70**, across community and inpatient TBI samples, and assessed an inpatient subgroup six months after rehabilitation discharge. Its validity and prognostic associations make it useful for retrieving supervision/support evidence. **Six-month follow-up is not a six-month eligibility rule**, and TBI-specific findings need separate consideration from stroke, SCI and orthopedic recovery. ([Soo et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20510982/))

Takei's Japanese disability study broadens the search beyond older-person insurance: **1,423 service recipients across 60 municipalities**, with physical, intellectual and mental disabilities, informed an assessment for service types and quantities. The abstract does not expose the precise age range or complete validation method. ([Takei et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18191276/)) PCANS provides an actual pediatric sentinel: **32 parents/carers of children aged 5–18 with acquired brain injury**, assessed against disability/function measures. It is preliminary support-need measurement, not a child entitlement rule. WP3 should distinguish ordinary age-related assistance from additional disability-related support rather than applying adult dependence scores without qualification. ([Soo et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18781503/))

The Thai hip-fracture paper supplies local prognostic context. The development cohort (**291 patients, 2010–2020**) and later validation cohort (**93 patients, 2021–2022**) totalled **384 patients aged at least 60** at Prince of Songkhla University; the predischarge model predicted three-month bedridden status with validation AUC **0.83**. Although described by its authors as external validation, the later cohort came from the **same hospital**, so it is temporal validation and does not establish generalizability across Thai sites. Bedridden status is a narrower endpoint than multidomain LTC need, and three-month dependence does not establish permanence. Use this paper to discuss assessment timing and prognosis, not to justify a Thai benefit threshold. ([Dissaneewate et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11049116/))

Taken together, these studies support considering current need and likely recovery as separate variables. A person can need continuing help while rehabilitation remains beneficial. Repeated assessment offers a more defensible research construct than inferring stable need from elapsed time alone. No study verified in this focused search validates the actual Thai IMC-to-LTC entry decision; that is a defined evidence gap for further searching, not proof that such evidence does not exist.

## The team should set scope before choosing outcomes

The proposed merged question is: **Which criteria, assessment instruments or decision algorithms identify sustained need for long-term care or support, and how have they been developed or evaluated, including among people leaving acute or intermediate rehabilitation care?** “Sustained” needs an agreed operational definition. It should not silently mean no recovery potential, institutional placement, or a particular six-month interval. Those are protocol-level decisions for the team.

| Proposed PECO element | Working specification for team discussion |
| --- | --- |
| Population | People of any age with functional, cognitive, behavioral, nursing or support needs; separate older people, working-age disability and children; identify postacute/rehabilitation subgroups |
| Exposure/index assessment | Criteria, tools, graded assessments or algorithms intended to determine LTC eligibility, sustained care need or entry after rehabilitation |
| Comparator | Independent assessment/adjudication, another operational rule, assessed care need, care time or usual decision process; no-comparator development studies considered separately |
| Outcomes | Eligibility/grade agreement; reliability and construct validity; support intensity/unmet need; later LTC entry and health outcomes; analyze these in separate strata |

The most consequential decision is whether B studies are eligible evidence or contextual design material. A narrow entitlement review is coherent, but loses much rehabilitation and pediatric measurement literature. A broader care-needs review is also coherent if the decision purposes remain distinct. Requiring **actual LTC entry or later health outcomes in every included study** would exclude direct instrument-development and reliability evidence. The team should resolve this before the main search rather than extending inclusion ad hoc after seeing results.

Actual eligibility rules also need a separate policy/manual stream. Historical validation papers cannot establish current age gates, duration requirements, exclusions, reassessment, appeals or financial eligibility. Japan's current manuals differ from the 2005 instrument description; older-person LTC and disability/child services follow distinct routes. ([MHLW certification manuals](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/hukushi_kaigo/kaigo_koureisha/nintei/index.html); [MHLW disability classification](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/hukushi_kaigo/shougaishahukushi/kubun/index.html)) Domains and workflow are plausible adaptation inputs; foreign cutoff numbers require Thai policy and validation work.

For the eventual prototype/provider survey, two supplemental measurement papers inform feasibility concerns. InterRAI's 12-country paired-assessment study found mean weighted κ **0.75** for common items in **783 people**, under trained-assessor conditions; reliability does not validate an entitlement threshold. ([Hirdes et al. 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6963-8-277)) The Flemish BelRAI study found approximately **11% of total-score variation between assessors** among 743 adults. This supports attention to assessor training and contextual influences, not an assumption of unbiased scores. ([Van Doren et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8617752/)) A provider acceptability/feasibility survey can examine usability, staffing, assessment time and difficult cases; it cannot by itself validate eligibility accuracy or choose the normative cutoff.

The extraction form should record the intended decision and actual policy use; instrument/version; population/age/diagnosis; home versus institution; assessment timing/time origin; pre-existing versus new dependence; domains including cognition, supervision and caregivers; assessor/mode/training; output and threshold; development method; reference-standard definition and independence; reliability, responsiveness, discrimination and calibration separately; internal, temporal or geographic validation; missingness, death and loss to follow-up; informal-care treatment; resource availability; and explicit Thai adaptation issues. Multiple papers from one instrument or cohort should be linked rather than counted as independent replications.

## Search blocks need known item checks

The exploratory work used web searches and primary PubMed, publisher, institutional and government pages in three strands: eligibility tools, Asian systems and postacute transitions. It did not run complete database searches, estimate retrieval sensitivity/precision or apply formal screening. Some sources exposed only abstracts; occasional CAPTCHA and publisher access failures limited full-text verification. The seed table preserves those distinctions. The studies are **ten unique papers across nine instrument/model families**, counting the MI Choice pair together; shared data or instrument lineage still needs full-text checking.

For the workshop, use three connected concept blocks as a starting proposal. The care/service block can include `long-term care`, `home care`, `community care`, `care services`, `disability services`, and `support needs`. The decision/measurement block can include `eligib*`, `certif*`, `care need*`, `needs assessment`, `level of care`, `care grade*`, `dependen*`, `screen*`, `classification`, `priorit*`, `valid*`, and `develop*`. The transition branch can combine `postacute`, `intermediate care`, `rehabilitation`, `discharge`, `hospital to home`, and `continuing care`. These are concepts, not a tested database-ready Boolean query. Database subject headings, field tags and syntax need adaptation with พี่รุ่ง.

Do not require the transition block in the entire Q1 search: doing so could omit general certification-development studies. Conversely, a separate transition/care-needs search can retrieve papers without “LTC” in the title. Do not impose an older-person population filter if all ages remains the agreed scope. Country/tool-name searching and backward/forward citation chasing should supplement, not replace, the main concept search.

Representative exact exploratory queries entered on 12 October were `MI Choice nursing home eligibility screening instrument validation Fries 2002`; `interRAI MAPLe validation Hirdes 2008 home care priority levels`; `Germany new assessment instrument long term care eligibility validation study 2015`; `Korea long term care eligibility assessment validation care time instrument study`; `Taiwan long term care eligibility assessment validation care needs instrument`; `Thailand long-term care eligibility Barthel study`; `"Resource Utilization Groups in transitional home care" 2023 DOI`; `"Northwick Park Care Needs Assessment" "validation"`; `"Validity and responsiveness of the Care and Needs Scale" pubmed`; and `"Development and Validation" "Paediatric Care and Needs Scale" "Soo" 2008`. The complete branch query logs remain in the three research notes. These exploratory queries are reproducibility context, not the proposed SR strategy.

The next concrete check is **known-item retrieval**. For each seed, confirm database indexing, then test whether the proposed search retrieves it without adding its title solely to force a hit. Record retrieved/not retrieved and the missing concept. Keep PCANS as a deliberate child sentinel and CANS/transitional RUG as transition sentinels even if later excluded from the main evidence review. A query retrieving all ten demonstrates known-item coverage only; it cannot establish overall sensitivity. Document full database strings, dates, hits and deduplication once the strategy is agreed, with any post hoc adjustments recorded.

Priority retrieval gaps are Michigan full methods/sample distributions; original Japanese statistical development reports; precise Takei age/reference-standard details; pediatric manuals; German and BelRAI development/threshold grey literature; and current Thai official LTC/IMC rules. No verified pediatric entitlement study or Thai IMC endpoint-validation rule was identified here. Language coverage should explicitly consider Japanese and Chinese source families, while country-specific databases and grey literature require a separate agreed search plan.

The immediate value of this seed set is an auditable distinction between **who qualifies, how much help is needed, who needs assessment first, and who is likely to enter a service**. WP3 can use that distinction to build a coherent review and avoid selecting an apparently accurate tool that answers the wrong policy question. The workshop should resolve the evidence strata and outcome requirements before refining the search; threshold choices and the meaning of sustained need remain team decisions.
