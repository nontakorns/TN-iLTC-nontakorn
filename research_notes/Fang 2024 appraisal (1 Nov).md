# Fang 2024: critical appraisal (1 Nov)

Fang W, Phung H, Olley R, Lee P. The Major Domains of Comprehensive Assessment Tools for Older Adults Requiring Home-Based Aged Care Services: A Systematic Review. *Healthcare* 2024;12(23):2468. doi:10.3390/healthcare12232468. PMC11641082.

Read from the full text via Europe PMC (1 Nov). Supplements S1–S3 were not read by Claude.

**Sources of each point:**
- "Text": a fact quoted from the paper.
- "Judgement": Claude's view, for Nontakorn to weigh.

## Major problems
1. **They excluded the studies that best describe tools** (Text, Table 1). Development, reliability and validity studies were excluded, along with studies of feasibility, quality or effectiveness of tools. (Judgement) For a review whose aim is to describe assessment tools, this removes the core evidence. What's left is mostly studies that *used* a measure for research.
2. **Research measures were mixed up with service assessments** (Text: RCTs, cohort and cross-sectional studies were included, with domains counted per study). (Judgement) "28% assessed physical function (handgrip, chair stand)" describes what researchers measured, not what home-care services assess when deciding care. The counts can't support "the major domains of home-care assessment".
3. **They counted studies, not tools** (Text: "Nine studies (28.1%)…"). (Judgement) A tool studied in many papers inflates its domains.
4. **The quality appraisal was never used** (Text: MMAT appears only in Methods and Supplement S3). It doesn't inform the results or the conclusions. Two further problems:
   - (Text) They averaged the two raters' scores instead of resolving disagreements.
   - (Text) They summed the 5 items into high/moderate/low. (Judgement) The MMAT authors advise against an overall score.
5. **No grey literature, English only, 2013 onwards** (Text). (Judgement) Governments' own assessment instruments, such as interRAI-HC as used by provinces and countries, are documented mostly in grey literature, so the real-world tools are under-represented. They admit this as a limitation, but it undermines the aim.

## Smaller problems
- **Not registered** (Text: "The review was not registered"). They also used PCC, the scoping-review framework, while calling the work a systematic review.
- **The gap is thin.** Figueiredo 2018 had already listed domains and instruments.
- **Possible factual error** (Text + Judgement). They call cut-offs of 11 and 7 on the MNA "heterogeneity". But 11/7 match the official MNA-Short Form cut-offs (0–14 scale: 12–14 normal, 8–11 at risk, 0–7 malnourished), so this looks like confusing the MNA with the MNA-SF. Check Swan et al. before citing this.
- **Editing slips** (Text). For example, "morbidity, self-care, and domestic life" where the heading is "Mobility". interRAI-HC is listed under "Products and Technology".
- **Conclusions are descriptive only** ("frequently assessed… future research should…"). Nothing concrete for practice or policy.

## Strengths (to be fair)
- Two reviewers screened independently, with disagreements resolved by consensus.
- The search was built with a librarian, across 5 databases plus reference lists.
- PRISMA flow diagram. Supplements include the full search strategy, extraction sheet and appraisal table.
- The ICF gives a recognised frame for sorting domains.

## Why it passed review (Judgement)
Peer review mostly checks that a paper reports its methods and admits its limitations. Fang does both: it's transparent about being unregistered, having no grey literature and being English-only. Design choices like excluding validation studies are easy to miss unless the reviewer knows the field.

## Lessons for WP3
- **Include development and validation studies.** These are the main source for "what evidence supports them" in Q1.
- **Count tools or systems, not papers.** Keep "how a service decides" separate from "what a research study measured". The two streams agreed 12 Oct (empirical studies + policy documents) handle this.
- **Grey literature is essential for Q1.** Eligibility rules live in government documents.
- **Use the appraisal in the synthesis** (e.g. say which findings rest on weak studies). Agreed 12 Oct: no numerical scores.
- **Register on PROSPERO**, as already planned.
- **Make the conclusion feed something concrete:** the evidence-to-design matrix, then the draft Thai criteria.
