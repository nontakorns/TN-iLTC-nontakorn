# Post-acute and rehabilitation transition evidence for TN-iLTC Q1+Q2

## Which primary papers assess continuing care need rather than simply predict institutional placement?

### Takeaway
The best adjacent evidence assesses the type, frequency, intensity, and hours of continuing support. These are useful for constructing and validating a needs assessment, but none of the papers below establishes a legal entitlement threshold for Thai LTC or validates an IMC-to-LTC decision at six months. Team decision required: whether such measurement studies meet the merged review's eligibility criteria.

### Cited Findings

1. **Turner-Stokes L, Tonge P, Nyein K, Hunter M, Nielson S, Robinson I. The Northwick Park Dependency Score (NPDS): a measure of nursing dependency in rehabilitation. Clin Rehabil. 1998;12(4):304–318. DOI 10.1191/026921598669173600; PMID 9744666.** Development plus reliability/concurrent validity study; UK inpatient rehabilitation. Evaluation cohorts: 23 inpatients with five senior nurses, then revised version tested in 21 with three nurses. Items encode basic care and special nursing needs using helpers/time, beyond generic ADL ability. Pairwise total-score correlations were 0.73–0.92; agreement within one item level 73–100%; basic care score inversely associated with Barthel. Abstract prints rho = 0.91 while calling it inverse: do not silently assign a positive/negative sign. Community translation had not yet been validated. **Read status: PubMed abstract only.** Strong historical development seed; small, specialist ward sample and trained raters limit transferability. — [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9744666/)

2. **Turner-Stokes L, Nyein K, Halliwell D. The Northwick Park Care Needs Assessment (NPCNA): a directly costable outcome measure in rehabilitation. Clin Rehabil. 1999;13(3):253–267. DOI 10.1191/026921599677787870; PMID 10392653.** Developed conversion from NPDS to community care timetable, weekly care hours, care package, and approximate costs. Prospective comparison in 35 cases, in a neurorehabilitation unit for younger people with brain injury. Independent structured interview was comparator; a third assessor costed both timetables using rules. Weekly-hours correlation rho 0.90; category/cost rho 0.73; no significant difference in hours. **Read status: publisher and PubMed abstracts only.** Directly useful for linking eligibility domains to care intensity; not an entitlement classifier, and UK package/cost assumptions need contextual adaptation. Publisher visible author heading omits Halliwell but PubMed lists her; use indexed citation above. — [Primary publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026921599677787870); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10392653/)

3. **Nyein K, Turner-Stokes L, Robinson I. The Northwick Park Care Needs Assessment (NPCNA): a measure of community care needs: sensitivity to change during rehabilitation. Clin Rehabil. 1999;13(6):482–491. DOI 10.1191/026921599674590637; PMID 10588534.** Prospective cohort of 39 consecutive brain injury patients in postacute rehabilitation for young patients with complex disability. NPCNA at admission/discharge; 28 assessed in hospital and home around discharge; 15 followed at three months in community. Median weekly care hours decreased 52→17 during inpatient rehabilitation (p<0.001), and improvement persisted at follow-up. Discharge assessment estimated community care hours reasonably; no significant relationship to FIM gain. **Read status: publisher abstract only.** Especially useful transition seed because it tests home transfer and responsiveness, not only ward validity. Tiny follow-up subset, short horizon, no sensitivity/specificity eligibility threshold, and no fixed six-month rule. — [Primary abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026921599674590637)

4. **Tate RL. Assessing support needs for people with traumatic brain injury: the Care and Needs Scale (CANS). Brain Inj. 2004;18(5):445–460. DOI 10.1080/02699050310001641183; PMID 15195793.** Development/validity study in 67 severe TBI survivors, 20–26 years after injury. Eight categories cover intermittent through continuous support. Fully independent: 28.4%; less-than-daily need: 46.3%; daily need: 25.4%. Correlation with Supervision Rating Scale 0.75; correlations with participation scales −0.46 to −0.85. Historical discharge variables classified long-term independence with 77% accuracy; neuropsychological disability was predictive. **Read status: PubMed abstract only.** Useful for avoiding an ADL-only view and capturing supervision/support even when help is not daily. Historical survivor sample, small n, no external validation or policy eligibility threshold; classification accuracy is not diagnostic sensitivity. — [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15195793/)

5. **Soo C, Tate RL, Aird V, Allaous J, Browne S, Carr B, Coulston C, Diffley L, Gurka J, Hummell J. Validity and responsiveness of the care and needs scale for assessing support needs after traumatic brain injury. Arch Phys Med Rehabil. 2010;91(6):905–912. DOI 10.1016/j.apmr.2009.11.033; PMID 20510982.** Two Sydney units; n=108 aged 16–70: community samples 38 and 30, inpatient sample 40 followed six months after rehabilitation discharge. Concurrent validity absolute correlations 0.43–0.68; differentiated injury severity and functional groups; discharge CANS predicted six-month functioning. **Read status: PubMed and publisher abstracts plus limited publisher snippets, not full text.** Best Q2 CANS seed: an explicit six-month *follow-up*, not six months since stroke/injury or exhaustion of recovery. Need full text for precise prediction/responsiveness coefficients, missing follow-up, and change interpretation. PubMed abstract has apparent typo “r(s)=46”; do not reproduce this as a valid coefficient. — [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20510982/); [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003999310001255)

6. **Soo C, Tate RL, Williams L, Waddingham S, Waugh MC. Development and validation of the Paediatric Care and Needs Scale (PCANS) for assessing support needs of children and youth with acquired brain injury. Dev Neurorehabil. 2008;11(3):204–214. DOI 10.1080/17518420802259498; PMID 18781503.** 32 parents/carers of children aged 5–18 with ABI; developmental tool yields overall, extent, and intensity support measures. Validation against VABS, Wee-FIM and KOSCHI. Support-intensity correlations −0.46 to −0.77; similar psychosocial domains correlated −0.64, versus dissimilar domains −0.29. **Read status: indexed primary abstract; publisher fetch failed.** Important all-ages seed: children's usual age-related support and injury-related additional support require distinct interpretation. Preliminary small validation, not LTC entitlement criteria or longitudinal entry rule. — [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18781503/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended extraction implication:** separate measured care need (helpers/hours, supervision, communication/behaviour, additional nursing tasks), available caregiver resources, and actual service destination. Distinct measurement results in NPDS/NPCNA and CANS support this distinction; merging them into one “LTC admission” outcome would obscure the construct being validated. — [NPDS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9744666/); [CANS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15195793/)
- Repeated needs assessment has stronger conceptual support than using elapsed time as evidence of permanence. The observed improvement during rehabilitation and at community follow-up supports responsiveness/reassessment, not a particular administrative stopping date. — [Nyein 1999](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026921599674590637); [Soo 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20510982/)

### Gaps
- No verified study in this focused search validated a decision rule at the end of Thai six-month IMC. Do not treat UK postacute rehabilitation or Australian TBI rehabilitation as equivalent to Thai IMC.
- Normative child support versus excess support, translation, training, software/licensing and Thai feasibility need full-text/manual follow-up.

## Which prognosis studies could inform a referral trigger or reassessment after hospital care?

### Takeaway
The strongest additional examples here are a Swiss discharge model combining current self-care with preadmission/social context, and a Thai temporally validated model predicting three-month bedridden status after hip fracture. Both have useful predictor domains but neither proves LTC clinical eligibility.

### Cited Findings

7. **Koch D, Schuetz P, Haubitz S, Kutz A, Mueller B, Weber H, Regez K, Conca A; Triage Study Group. Improving the post-acute care discharge score (PACD) by adding patients' self-care abilities: a prospective cohort study. PLoS One. 2019;14(3):e0214194. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0214194; PMID 30921356.** Swiss single-centre prospective cohort; 1,342 medical and 402 neurological patients; inclusion >16 years, ED admissions February–October 2013. PACD assesses medical problems, age, preadmission ADL/IADL and household helper availability; self-care index adds current mobility, hygiene, dressing, intake, excretion, cognition. Actual PAC facility discharge occurred in 150 medical/94 neurological patients. Combined AUC rose 0.77→0.83 and 0.68→0.78 respectively. **Read status: full text.** Excluded nursing-home admissions, interhospital transfers, inpatient deaths and incomplete scores; single centre, model updated in existing data. Outcome is broad postacute destination, not sustained LTC need; rehab and intermediate care must be separated from nursing-home residence in extraction. Useful trigger example if prediction studies are team-approved. — [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6438596/)

8. **Dissaneewate K, Dissaneewate P, Orapiriyakul W, Kritsaneephaiboon A, Chewakidakarn C. Development and Validation of Two-Step Prediction Models for Postoperative Bedridden Status in Geriatric Intertrochanteric Hip Fractures. Diagnostics. 2024;14(8):804. DOI 10.3390/diagnostics14080804; PMID 38667450.** Retrospective cohort at Prince of Songkhla University, Thailand; age ≥60, low-energy isolated fractures treated with cephalomedullary nail. n=384: development 291 (2010–2020), later validation 93 (2021–2022), same hospital. Admission model: age, dementia, ASA and prefracture ambulation; predischarge model adds ambulation. Three-month bedridden outcome in 22%; later-cohort AUC 0.72 vs 0.83 (95% CI 0.74–0.92 for predischarge); calibration slope 0.89 (0.51–1.35). **Read status: full text.** Authors call it external validation; technically temporal same-centre validation, not geographically independent. EMR outcome, complete cases, excludes follow-up loss; only three-month mobility endpoint. High local relevance as prognosis example; no LTC criterion/entry threshold or evidence that dependency persists permanently. — [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11049116/)

**Supporting papers (lower priority than eight seeds above):**

- **Saab A, Glass-Kaastra S, Young GB. Discharge Destination from a Rehabilitation Unit After Acute Ischemic Stroke. Can J Neurol Sci. 2019;46(2):209–215. DOI 10.1017/cjn.2018.386.** Retrospective 2005–2015 Canadian stroke rehabilitation records. Predicts home versus broadly defined LTC (assisted living through complete ADL care). Final associations: age, bowel incontinence, nonhome prestroke residence, right-sided lesion, absent caregiver. Many functional/cognitive variables were univariable associations only. **Read status: publisher text accessible, tables not fully inspected.** n=227 is stated in the Canadian guideline evidence table but not independently confirmed from the primary table in this run; keep sample-size field pending. No verified AUC/calibration/external validation. Useful social-context paper, but institutional destination reflects service and caregiver context as well as patient need. — [Primary article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-neurological-sciences/article/discharge-destination-from-a-rehabilitation-unit-after-acute-ischemic-stroke/079515069576C670BF89638B56EEA9AA)
- **Turner-Stokes L, Lafeuillee G, Francis R, Nayar M, Nair A. Functional outcomes and cost-efficiency of specialist in-patient rehabilitation following spinal cord injury: a multi-centre national cohort analysis from the UK Rehabilitation Outcomes Collaborative (UKROC). Disabil Rehabil. 2022;44(19):5603–5611 (online 2021). DOI 10.1080/09638288.2021.1946603; PMID 34282991.** Prospective registry data analysed retrospectively, England 2012–2019; 2,506/3,321 admissions had complete paired UK FIM+FAM and NPDS. Both function and dependency improved; 72.5% discharged home. **Read status: primary abstract.** Application of need measures rather than new rule development. Includes adult SCI and a majority reported under 65; abstract's “1344 (56%)” does not align with denominator 2506, so retain reported age percentage with caution or obtain full denominator. Useful all-adult SCI/care-hours evidence; no untreated comparator, no postdischarge persistence validation. — [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34282991/)
- **Conca A et al. Extension and Validation of the Self-care Index to Predict Transfer to a Post-acute Care Institution in Internal Medicine Patients. Int J Health Prof. 2022;9(1):59–66. DOI 10.2478/ijhp-2022-0005.** Later SPI model update, randomly split train/validation, age and sex added; validation AUC 0.837 versus total-score AUC 0.808. Outcome pools temporary care, transient nursing, health resort treatment, rehabilitation and nursing home. Original ≤32 threshold sensitivity/specificity 64.3%/83.7%; altered ≤36 81.8%/68.4%. **Read status: publisher full text.** Useful illustration of threshold trade-offs; needs external validation. This is not a separate independent Swiss validation of the 2019 combined PACD+SPI model. — [Primary full text](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/ijhp-2022-0005?tab=article)

### Inferences
- Thresholds optimized for PAC destination or bedridden status cannot be adopted directly as LTC eligibility thresholds. They identify different outcomes at different time origins and horizons. — [Koch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6438596/); [Dissaneewate 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11049116/)
- Separate pre-existing need from new disability and discharge status. Current assessment adds predictive value to baseline history, but recovery/service context remains unresolved. — [Koch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6438596/); [Dissaneewate 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11049116/)

### Gaps
- No verified prognosis threshold in these papers balances Thai false-negative exclusion from care versus false-positive referral. This value judgment belongs to the team/policy stakeholders.
- Outcomes beyond mobility (behaviour, supervision, cognition, unmet needs, caregiver capacity) are not jointly represented by the Thai hip-fracture model.

## What should the team extract, and what was searched?

### Takeaway
Before protocol finalization, decide whether the review includes (a) entitlement/clinical eligibility rules, (b) care-need measurement validation, and (c) prognosis/referral studies as separate strata. My recommended seed set intentionally exposes these boundaries; this is focused research, not a database-complete systematic review.

### Cited Findings
- Tool outputs differ: care hours (NPCNA), support intensity/frequency (CANS/PCANS), destination (PACD), and future bedridden status (Thai model). These papers must not contribute to a pooled diagnostic-accuracy estimate for a common “LTC eligibility” construct without a justified common reference standard. — [NPCNA](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/026921599677787870); [CANS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20510982/); [PCANS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18781503/); [PACD](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6438596/); [Thai model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11049116/)

### Inferences
Recommended extraction columns: jurisdiction/care system; age/diagnosis; admission pathway; baseline vs new disability; assessment timing and time origin; rehab availability; predictor domains; tool developer/intended user; output/threshold; outcome/reference-standard definition; follow-up interval; care at home vs institutional care; death/loss/missingness; development vs internal/temporal/geographic validation; discrimination and calibration separately; reliability/responsiveness separately; whether explicit entitlement or clinical entry decision exists; caregiver context; Thai adaptation needs.

No claim of full-text verification unless explicitly stated above. Search engine coverage cannot demonstrate that a recent relevant paper does not exist.
