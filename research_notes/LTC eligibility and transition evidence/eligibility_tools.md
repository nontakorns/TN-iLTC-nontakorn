# International LTC eligibility and care-needs tools

## Which primary papers are useful seeds for merged Q1+Q2?

### Takeaway
Seven defensible primary seeds are listed below, with different decision purposes. The Michigan pair most directly addresses medical/service eligibility; the others assess measurement, prioritization, resource allocation, or fairness and should not automatically be treated as equivalent eligibility-validation studies.

### Cited Findings

#### 1. Direct seed: MI Choice development
- **Citation:** Fries BE, Shugarman LR, Morris JN, Simon SE, James M. A screening system for Michigan's home- and community-based long-term care programs. *Gerontologist*. 2002;42(4):462–474. DOI **10.1093/geront/42.4.462**; PMID **12145374**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12145374/)
- **Purpose/design/reference standard:** Development of a screen for publicly funded Michigan HCBS eligibility and appropriate level of care. MDS-HC client characteristics predict expert care-manager ratings across five levels: nursing home, home care, intermittent personal care, homemaker, information/referral. Approximately 30 characteristics produced κ=.62 against expert opinion; ADL/IADL-only systems had κ<.40. This is agreement with expert judgement, not proof of improved patient outcomes or objective appropriateness. **Verified:** PubMed metadata and abstract; publisher full text could not be opened. **Sample size and exact age distribution remain unverified.** [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12145374/)

#### 2. Direct seed: telephone MI Choice validation
- **Citation:** Fries BE, James M, Hammer SS, Shugarman LR, Morris JN. Is telephone screening feasible? Accuracy and cost-effectiveness of identifying people medically eligible for home- and community-based services. *Gerontologist*. 2004;44(5):680–688. DOI **10.1093/geront/44.5.680**; PMID **15498843**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15498843/)
- **Design/outcomes:** Michigan telephone screens compared with in-person MDS-HC assessments; weighted κ assessed agreement. Telephone recommendations matched only marginally. Telephone respondents were classified as more impaired than in-person assessment in 27% of cases, less impaired in 6%. The authors support broad targeting to reduce unnecessary visits but explicitly reject telephone screening alone for final medical eligibility or a specific level of care. **Verified:** primary abstract; full-text methods/n not reviewed. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15498843/)

#### 3. Adjacent seed: MAPLe priority/risk algorithm
- **Citation:** Hirdes JP, Poss JW, Curtin-Telegdi N. The Method for Assigning Priority Levels (MAPLe): A new decision-support system for allocating home care resources. *BMC Med*. 2008;6:9. DOI **10.1186/1741-7015-6-9**; PMID **18366782**. [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-6-9)
- **Design/population:** Decision-tree derivation from 4,836 routine RAI-HC assessments in 14 Ontario centres, 1999–2001; validation across multiple jurisdictions in six countries. Five priority levels combine clinical features including ADL impairment, cognition and behaviour. Outcomes: caregiver distress, judgement that client is better off elsewhere, and 90-day nursing-home admission. Separate Ontario follow-up found one-year admission of 4.8% at low priority versus 47.4% at very high priority. This prioritizes need/risk and supports judgement; it is not an automated entitlement rule. **Verified:** publisher full-text methods/results; nursing-home follow-up outside Ontario unavailable. Note derivation results table reports n=4,835 although methods report 4,836. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-6-9)

#### 4. Adjacent seed: home-care resource classification validation
- **Citation:** Poss JW, Hirdes JP, Fries BE, McKillop I, Chase M. Validation of Resource Utilization Groups version III for Home Care (RUG-III/HC): evidence from a Canadian home care jurisdiction. *Med Care*. 2008;46(4):380–387. DOI **10.1097/MLR.0b013e31815c3b6c**; PMID **18362817**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18362817/)
- **Design/population/outcome:** Ontario validation linking RAI-HC classifications to 13-week billing episodes, **29,921 episodes**. Combined formal/informal service cost was the reference outcome, valuing informal care at approximately half replacement-worker cost. Explained variance: 37.3% combined cost versus 20.5% formal cost alone; >56% of cases were in reduced physical functions. Professional-discipline costs were less well explained. **Purpose:** case mix/resource allocation, not eligibility. **Verified:** primary abstract only; detailed age ranges, attrition and full-text bias assessment pending. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18362817/)

#### 5. Adjacent seed, especially useful for IMC bridge: transitional home care
- **Citation:** Bolster-Foucault C, Holyoke P. Resource Utilization Groups in transitional home care: validating the RUG-III/HC case-mix system in hospital-to-home care programs. *BMC Health Serv Res*. 2023;23:1324. DOI **10.1186/s12913-023-10150-1**; PMID **38037101**. [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-10150-1)
- **Design/population:** Retrospective cohort, **1,680 completed episodes** (2019–2022), eight Ontario 10–16-week programs, one provider. InterRAI-HC at admission and billing data classified 23 RUG groups using clinical complexity and ADL/IADL dependence; operational sample n=1,575 after duration outlier exclusion. Complete billing records: n=1,251 full sample, n=1,184 operational sample. Combined paid/unpaid care-time variance explained was 21.88% full and 23.34% operational. About 79% were clinically complex or reduced physical functions. **Limitation:** convenience sample and incomplete billing; actual delivered resources reflect service organisation. **Verified:** full-text methods/results. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-10150-1)

#### 6. Adjacent seed: reliability across LTC and post-acute settings
- **Citation:** Hirdes JP, Ljunggren G, Morris JN, Frijters DHM, Finne Soveri H, Gray L, Björkgren M, Gilgen R. Reliability of the interRAI suite of assessment instruments: a 12-country study of an integrated health information system. *BMC Health Serv Res*. 2008;8:277. DOI **10.1186/1472-6963-8-277**; PMID **19115991**. [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6963-8-277)
- **Design/population/outcomes:** **783 people**, blinded paired assessments within 72 hours, trained assessors in 12 countries. Five instruments: HC, LTCF, post-acute, mental health, palliative. Weighted κ mean for 161 common items=.75; specialised-item means=.63–.73; >60% of items exceeded .70. Australia, Japan and Korea participated. Pilot sites chosen by participating researchers limit representativeness; reliability does not validate a benefit threshold. **Verified:** primary abstract and publisher methods/discussion. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6963-8-277)

#### 7. Supportive direct-context seed: BelRAI assessment fairness
- **Citation:** Van Doren S, De Coninck D, Hermans K, Declercq A. Examining Care Assessment Scores of Community-Dwelling Adults in Flanders, Belgium: The Role of Socio-Psychological and Assessor-Related Factors. *Int J Environ Res Public Health*. 2021;18(22):11845. DOI **10.3390/ijerph182211845**; PMID **34831601**. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34831601/)
- **Design/population/outcomes:** Cross-sectional **743 community-dwelling adults** with chronic disease/disability nested in **92 assessors**, 2019. BelRAI domains: IADL, ADL, cognition, psychological and behavioural problems; total 0–30. The screener is used for regional care-budget eligibility and escalation to comprehensive assessment. About 11% of total-score variance clustered between assessors. Social-context associations included language proficiency and caregiver presence. No independent gold standard or eligibility sensitivity/specificity was tested, and age/gender/education were not collected. **Verified:** PMC-indexed full-text sections plus PubMed metadata; direct PMC opening later encountered CAPTCHA. [Methods/results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8617752/)

### Inferences
- **Workshop ordering:** read MI Choice 2002 and 2004 first; MAPLe next to expose the difference between eligibility and prioritization; Bolster-Foucault 2023 next for the IMC-to-home-care bridge. Add interRAI reliability and BelRAI fairness if assessment measurement properties are in scope. Poss 2008 is a useful resource-allocation comparator. These are candidate seeds, not final included studies.
- **Thai transferability:** MI Choice offers a testable screen→assessment workflow; it does not supply Thai entitlement criteria. MAPLe suggests cognition, behaviour and caregiver strain should be considered alongside physical function. Transitional RUG evidence shows long-stay classifications should be retested in post-acute pathways. BelRAI highlights assessor training and social context; Thai language/local assessor studies would still be required. These are research inferences, not recommendations to import scores or cutoffs.

### Gaps
- Exact n/age distributions in MI Choice 2002 and 2004 require full-text retrieval. No child validation seed was verified in this strand; all-ages eligibility in the protocol must not imply these tools were validated in children.
- Complete operational-eligibility validation of BelRAI is largely in Dutch grey reports, not the 2021 association paper above.

## What distinction should guide the protocol and extraction?

### Takeaway
Eligibility, assessment priority, resource intensity and future placement are separate targets. Their reference standards and performance measures should be extracted separately, with inclusion boundaries agreed by the WP3 team.

### Cited Findings
- MI Choice compares classification to expert care-manager level-of-care judgements; its follow-up telephone study finds that remote screening should not itself determine final medical eligibility. [Development](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12145374/); [telephone validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15498843/)
- MAPLe was derived against three adverse-outcome/need markers and intended as a decision aid; RUG-III/HC validates against delivered cost/time, a different target. [MAPLe](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-6-9); [RUG validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18362817/)
- **Background, not a standalone quantitative validation seed:** Büscher A, Wingenfeld K, Schaeffer D. Determining eligibility for long-term care—lessons from Germany. *Int J Integr Care*. 2011;11:e019. DOI **10.5334/ijic.584**; PMID **21949486**. Development/policy account of an eligibility assessment designed around dependence on personal assistance rather than time spent delivering physical care, with attention to cognitive impairments and German social-insurance requirements. No independent quantitative validation estimates were verified from this paper. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3178799/)
- **Grey-literature lead, not yet extracted:** Vermeulen B, Van Eenoo L, Vanneste D, Declercq A. *Naar een getrapt gebruik van BelRAI met de BelRAI Screener*. Leuven: LUCAS; March 2015. Verified institutional authorship/title/date; detailed n/reference standards/results not reviewed. [KU Leuven record](https://soc.kuleuven.be/ceso/staff/00003876/view?fromnr=51&pubsonpage=50&pubtype=report&sortby=scdate)
- Flemish government confirms BelRAI Screener is operationally used for care-budget entitlement, including expanded use from March 2026. Operational use itself is not evidence of independent diagnostic validity. [Department of Care](https://www.departementzorg.be/nl/belrai-vlaanderen)

### Inferences
- **Team decision:** whether the review includes tools that only assess needs, prioritize assessment, allocate intensity, or predict institutionalization. A broader scope can be coherent if these purposes form distinct evidence strata.
- Suggested extraction: intended decision; actual policy use; domains; instrument version; assessor and mode; population and setting; acute/post-acute vs persistent need; development method; comparator/reference standard; measurement reliability; discrimination/calibration; eligibility agreement; implementation/equity; treatment of informal care; follow-up and reassessment. Do not let κ or explained cost variance stand in for eligibility validity.
- For Q2, requiring an LTC-entry outcome alone could miss the transitional resource study and cross-setting reliability study. The team should decide whether these are eligible evidence or only contextual design papers.

### Gaps
- German-language original validation reports and Dutch BelRAI development/threshold reports need targeted grey-literature retrieval.
- No defensible UK NHS Continuing Healthcare DST psychometric-validation primary paper or Australian integrated-assessment eligibility-validation paper was verified in this rapid strand. This is a search gap, not proof that none exists.

## How was this research performed, and what remains?

### Takeaway
This is a targeted seed-finding exercise through 12 October 2026, not an exhaustive database search. Primary PubMed/publisher sources were preferred; inaccessible full text and unverified details are explicitly marked.

### Cited Findings
- Relevant additional primary lead: Sinn CJ et al. Implementation evaluation of a stepped approach to home care assessment using interRAI systems in Ontario, Canada. 2022. DOI **10.1111/hsc.13784**. Evaluates actual versus planned contact-screen/comprehensive-assessment pathways; do not confuse it with eligibility accuracy. Full n and complete author/citation metadata remain unverified here. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/hsc.13784)

### Inferences
- Next searches should deliberately include country/tool names and grey literature. Backward citation chasing from MI Choice/MAPLe, German development, and BelRAI reports should be productive. A title containing neither eligibility nor transition may still describe an operational assessment pathway.

### Gaps
- **Access limitations:** intermittent PMC/PubMed CAPTCHA; unsuccessful publisher access for Michigan papers and MDPI. Search-indexed PMC sections supplied partial full-text verification for BelRAI. No paywalls bypassed. No local PDF mining was performed.
