## 1. What the project is

- **Name:** TN-iLTC — Thailand Integrated Long-Term Care Framework. Official Thai title on file: แบบข้อเสนอโครงการวิจัย (สวรส.). Reviewer-suggested new title was accepted on 8 Oct [Agreed].
- **Funder:** สวรส. (HSRI), FY2570. 8-month health policy and systems research (HPSR) project. สวรส. reviewers listed 6 months; the team kept 8 months.
- **PI:** ผศ.นพ.หฤษฎ์ ปัณณะรัส — **write "Aj Harisd" in English** (not "Harit"). ภาควิชาเวชศาสตร์ป้องกันและสังคม, Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital.
- **Core idea:** Strengthen Thai LTC by using *need-based eligibility* and the *care transition IMC → LTC → palliative care (PC)* as a **policy tracer**, adapting the **WHO Global Standards for LTC (consultation draft)** to Thailand.
- **The shift:** from dependency-based (Barthel-led) eligibility to need-based eligibility that weighs recovery potential, sustained care needs, carer context and burden, complexity, and patient/family goals — reassessed whenever things change.
- **Why:** LTC acts as an "end-of-line" service (you enter only after losing ADL). Early decline (frailty, deconditioning, malnutrition, early cognitive decline) doesn't show on Barthel, so reversible needs are missed and *become* permanent dependency.
- **Same-Barthel example (explainer):** two people with Barthel 8 — one lives with a capable daughter at home all day, the other alone with a frail spouse. Same dependency, very different needs.

### Work packages

| WP | Question | Kind of work | Output | Lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WP1 | Where is Thai LTC against WHO's 7 components / 34 standards? | Descriptive | System map + gap matrix; situation review per component | พญ.ณัฐณิชา มานะบริบูรณ์ (พี่เมจิ) |
| WP2 | Which standards are relevant and feasible for Thailand? | Evaluative | Modified Delphi (2 rounds, 35–45 people in proposal) → priority matrix, Foundation → Development → Comprehensive phasing | Team (no named lead) |
| WP3 | Who qualifies for LTC, at what level, and when to move IMC → LTC? | Normative | SR → evidence-to-design matrix → draft criteria → provider survey → policy options | **นพ.นนทกร (Nontakorn / หม่อน)** |

- WP1 and WP3 run **in parallel from the start**; outputs are two blocks: WP1 → WP2, and WP3 separately [Agreed 8 Oct; restated 27 Oct].
- **Adaptation Matrix:** each WHO standard gets a priority and phase. The *non-deferrable floor* (dignity, autonomy, protection from abuse, carer recognition) is not phased.
- **WHO GS frame:** 7 components (home- and community-based care, LTC facilities, unpaid carers, workforce, financing, governance, quality monitoring) + 5 foundational principles (rights-based, person-centred integrated care, carer recognition, ageing in the right place, quality). 34 standards, ~114 quality statements.
- **WHO does not give clinical triggers** — it leaves clinical guidance to other sources. That's why WP3 exists.
- **Conceptual framework:** WHO 7 components = skeleton; patient journey ICOPE/prevention → IMC → LTC → PC = tracer; principles = ethics floor.

### Objectives (proposal)
Overall: policy proposals for Thai LTC + a prototype "need-based eligibility and integrated care transition framework".
1. Gap analysis vs WHO GS (WP1)
2. Relevance/feasibility via Delphi (WP2)
3. Prioritisation and phasing (WP2)
4. Need-based eligibility + IMC → LTC triggers (WP3)
5. Non-cancer LTC → PC triggers and pathway (WP3) — *now dropped from the SR, see §4*
6. Policy implications and pilot options (WP3)

### Knowledge gaps the proposal claims
No mapping of Thai LTC vs WHO GS; no relevance/feasibility/order assessment; no systematically developed need-based eligibility framework; no clear IMC → LTC → PC transition points; no stakeholder consensus.

## 4. WP3 — scope and research question

### Original three questions (pre-8 Oct)
- Q1: assessment domains/criteria LTC systems use for eligibility and care level → entry into LTC
- Q2: factors showing an IMC patient will need sustained LTC → IMC → LTC trigger
- Q3: tools identifying palliative needs in non-cancer patients, especially in LTC → LTC → PC trigger

### Prior-SR check [Scan]
- Q3 heavily reviewed: two 2025 umbrella reviews (non-malignant chronic conditions: 7 SRs, 35 tools; older adults: 11 SRs, 29 instruments). Main tools SPICT, NECPAL, GSF-PIG, surprise question. Gap: almost nothing in LTC/home care or LMICs. File: Background/Healthcare 2026 - Palliative Care Needs Assessment Tools (umbrella review).pdf.
- Q2 partly: Harrison 2017, Burton 2017, 2024 review of prediction models — outcome is nursing-home admission, not Thai home-based LTC.
- Q1 barely: no SR. Mostly policy comparisons (JAMDA 2024 — 27 countries; World Bank toolkit; Gateway LTC Policy Explorer; OECD Health WP 181 (2025)).

### Q1 — locked wording
> **What eligibility criteria and assessment tools do long-term care systems use to decide who enters LTC and at what care level, including people moving from intermediate or post-acute care, and what evidence supports them?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **P** | People of any age being assessed for LTC, incl. people leaving IMC / post-acute care. Search: P1 (LTC) OR P2 (IMC/post-acute) |
| **E** | Criteria, assessment instruments, scoring/grading systems, decision algorithms |
| **C** | Not required; record any comparison tool / expert judgement |

### Agreed refinements (12 Oct, after ChatGPT critique rounds)
- **Two streams, split by what a document contributes, not who published it:** empirical review (how instruments were developed/evaluated) + policy mapping (rules systems use: thresholds, duration, reassessment, appeals). Both feed the evidence-to-design matrix (JBI text-and-policy approach).
- Screening rule: performance/prognostic outcomes are extracted from otherwise eligible studies; reporting them alone does not make a study eligible.
- Appraisal: every empirical study appraised by design and contribution (incl. development studies — how domains chosen, whether cognitive impairment / younger disability / carer needs were represented, whether items were service-driven); policy docs checked for authority, currency, scope, evidential basis. **No numerical scores.**

## 7. Thai baseline facts (for WP3 and WP1)

- **NHSO 2567 LTC dependency** = any of: **Barthel ≤11, PPS ≤50%, or dementia FAST moderate+ / GDS 5–7** [Rama p.114]. So Thailand already has a PPS-based palliative route; what's missing is a non-cancer needs-identification trigger (SPICT/NECPAL type).
- **IMC:** entry Barthel <15 (or ≥15 with ≥2 impairments); 4 conditions (stroke, TBI, SCI, hip fracture); ~6-month window; discharge at BI 20; BI <11 → refer to LTC [UHC report]. Inconsistency: LTC uses ≤11, IMC referral uses <11; report says discharge at BI "เกิน 20" (max is 20).
- Only ~0.9% of 2567 LTC entrants came via IMC (3,113 of 343,784) — IMC → LTC is a minor route; consider hospital-discharge and community entry too.
- LTC: NHSO/LAO-funded, 4 dependency groups, yearly care plan by care manager. LTC unit cost 10,442 THB/person/year; actual spend ~13–15k.
- NHSO moved from paying per registration to paying per approved care plan (only ~20% of registrants had a plan) [Rama p.252].
- Siloed baskets: IMC patients can't draw LTC consumables in first 6 months; palliative payment 1,000 baht × 6 months then convert to LTC; care plans approved quarterly/yearly so intensity can't step up [Rama p.250–252].
- **Gaming:** ADL lowered at entry and frozen at exit to keep benefits [Rama Theme 3.5] — don't use NHSO/3C ADL data to validate cut-offs.
- NHSO task force (2565) 5-group stratification by homebound/bedbound, confusion, complications, PPS 50/40/30/<30; ~60,600–174,800 baht/person-year [Rama Table 20] — trace primary source before citing.
- Dependency counts don't reconcile: NSO ~338,000 dependent elders (2567) vs NHSO LTC claims ~541,000 (2567) and ~644,000 (2568).
- Carers: 91–94% family, median age ~58–61, ~10% quit work, ~83% untrained [Rama Table 26]. Workforce 2567: 106,650 CG, 20,220 CM.

### WHO anchors for WP3
QS 1.2 (multidimensional needs assessment for care planning; interRAI-HC example, Box 5), **QS 20.1** (eligibility: standardised function + cognition, minimum duration; prioritise most severe when funds limited), QS 20.2 (graded benefits; dementia caveat on ADL-only tools), QS 20.3 (periodic review), QS 3.2 and 29.2 (transitions), Std 30 (palliative). Std 9 is carers, not recipients. WHO defines LTC as significant and sustained loss of intrinsic capacity; earlier decline belongs to ICOPE — **tension with the proposal's early-decline argument** (decide whether early decline sits inside eligibility or in a pre-LTC gate). Eligibility gate (QS 20.1) ≠ care-needs assessment (QS 1.2); the proposal's 11 minimum domains merge them [Proposed: split into a short gate + fuller assessment].
