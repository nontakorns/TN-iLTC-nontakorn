**Speaker notes:** Q1 in PCC format for a scoping review. Population: older adults assessed for publicly funded long-term care. Concept: eligibility and needs-assessment criteria: which domains are assessed, which instruments, what thresholds, who assesses, how often people are reassessed, and how the result links to care level or benefits. **Speaker notes:** Q2 in PEO format for a prognostic-factor systematic review. Population: adults 60 and over in intermediate or post-acute care, or discharged after acute illness (stroke, hip fracture, TBI, SCI, medical admissions). Outcome: sustained care need or entry to LTC, home-based or institutional, at 3 months or more. Whether to widen beyond IMC is decision 4.

Their population: adults 18+ with advanced non-malignant chronic conditions. ## Slide 7

Q3 base review

Karagkounis et al. · Healthcare 2026 · Umbrella review

7

6

Gap: weak psychometric evidence

**Speaker notes:** The base for Q3 is the umbrella review by Karagkounis, Connor, Papadatou and Bellali (Healthcare 2026;14:46, published 24 Dec 2025; PROSPERO CRD42024553053; JBI method). Four databases (Cochrane, CINAHL, PubMed, PsycINFO) were searched to 30 June 2025. Seven systematic reviews met the criteria and together identified 35 unique needs-assessment tools. Five tools combine general and disease-specific indicators (SPICT, GSF-PIG, QUICK GUIDE, NECPAL, P-CaRes); four cover physical, psychological, social and spiritual domains (PC-NAT, SPEED, NAT, IPOS). Psychometric data were available for only six tools across three reviews. The authors conclude NAT:PD-HF is promising for heart failure, and SPICT and NECPAL are widely used and adapted but need more psychometric testing. Our update adds LTC, home care and Asian settings, and the period after June 2025.
