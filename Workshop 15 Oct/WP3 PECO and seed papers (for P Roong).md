# WP3 Q1 (merged Q1 + Q2): PECO and 10 seed papers
For the search-strategy workshop, Thu 15 Oct 2569

## Review question
Which criteria, assessment tools or algorithms are used to decide who is eligible for long-term care (LTC), and at what care level, including for people leaving intermediate or post-acute care? What evidence supports them?

## PECO
| | |
|---|---|
| **P** | People of any age assessed for LTC or ongoing support, including people leaving intermediate/post-acute care (IMC) |
| **E** | Criteria, assessment tools, grading systems or algorithms used to decide LTC eligibility or care level |
| **C** | Not required. Record if present (another tool, expert judgment, independent assessment) |
| **O** | Main: domains assessed, decision rules/cut-offs, the eligibility or care-level result. Supporting: reliability, validity, later outcomes (LTC entry, decline, death), extracted if reported, not required for inclusion |

The search is built from P and E only.

## 10 seed papers
| # | Paper | Download | Why it fits WP3 Q1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tsutsui & Muramatsu 2005, J Am Geriatr Soc. Japan LTCI care-needs certification | https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2005.53175.x (PubMed 15743300) | Describes how Japan's national certification algorithm was built and graded into care levels. The clearest example of an eligibility system in Asia. |
| 2 | Fries et al. 2002, Gerontologist. MI Choice screening system, Michigan | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12145374/ | Develops a screen for who gets publicly funded home care, and at what level. It tests the screen against care managers' decisions. |
| 3 | Fries et al. 2004. Telephone vs in-person MI Choice screening | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15498843/ | Shows that a quick screen is not enough for the final eligibility decision. This informs a screen-then-full-assessment workflow for Thailand. |
| 4 | Kim et al. 2013. Korean LTCI Motor Impairment Scale | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3713298/ | Tests one part of Korea's certification tool against actual care time. It worked in facilities but not at home. |
| 5 | Tsugihashi et al. 2021 (ZEVIOUS). Japanese care levels vs WHODAS-12 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33393206/ | Checks whether Japan's certified care levels match people's real functioning. A validity test of an eligibility system in use. |
| 6 | Van Doren et al. 2021, IJERPH. BelRAI care scores, Flanders | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8617752/ | BelRAI scores decide eligibility for a regional care budget, across all adult ages. The scores vary by assessor, which matters for fairness. |
| 7 | Hirdes et al. 2008, BMC Med. MAPLe priority algorithm | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1741-7015-6-9 | Ranks who needs services most urgently and predicts nursing-home entry. It helps separate "priority" from "eligibility". |
| 8 | Bolster-Foucault & Holyoke 2023, BMC Health Serv Res. RUG-III/HC in transitional care | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-10150-1 | Tests a care-level classification in hospital-to-home transitional programmes. The closest match to IMC → LTC (old Q2). |
| 9 | Turner-Stokes et al. 1999, Clin Rehabil. Northwick Park Care Needs Assessment | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10392653/ | Converts care needs at the end of rehabilitation into a community care package (hours per week). The kind of assessment needed when people leave IMC. |
| 10 | Takei et al. 2008. Japan disability support-needs assessment | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18191276/ | Assesses service need for people with physical, intellectual and mental disabilities. It covers working-age people, so it tests our all-ages scope. |

Optional child test case: Soo et al. 2008, PCANS (children with brain injury), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18781503/
